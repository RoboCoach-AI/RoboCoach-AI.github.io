System overview

From imagined execution to targeted improvement

RoboCoach connects two closed loops: an execution loop that routes modular experts through an imagined rollout, and a coaching loop that converts consistent first-timeout diagnoses into a focused data request.

  1. 01

    Route

    Identify the first unfinished subtask from the instruction, current observation, and completed stages. Then dispatch its matching expert.
  2. 02

    Imagine

    CoachWorld rolls the routed experts forward in closed loop and predicts the full visual outcome.
  3. 03

    Diagnose

    The progress judge scores the observed video prefix. Completion advances the route. The first missed deadline identifies the active subtask–expert pair.
  4. 04

    Improve

    Collect targeted demonstrations, update only the selected LoRA, and return it to the expert bank.

Execution loopThe router is queried at initialization and after each judge-confirmed completion. The active expert acts inside CoachWorld. Each predicted video chunk supplies the observation for its next decision.

Coaching loopAcross imagined trials, consistent first-timeout diagnoses determine where the next demonstration budget goes. Only selected expert LoRAs are updated. The world model, router, judge, and VLA backbone remain fixed.

EvaluationThree coaching rounds add 100 accepted subtask demonstrations per round in simulation and 50 on hardware. Within each domain, comparisons match demonstration budgets, optimization, replay exposure, and evaluation trials. We report complete-task success across four budget checkpoints.

Predictive model

CoachWorld: our camera-aware action-conditioned world model across embodiments

CoachWorld starts from Wan2.2 TI2V-5B and predicts future video from sparse visual history and future robot states. A shared two-slot state contract supports Franka and bimanual systems. Calibrated intrinsics and extrinsics ground state conditioning in each camera view.

CoachWorld sustains closed-loop interaction for over a minute. The longest example below runs for 85.8 seconds. Predicted observations feed back into the policy as it continues to act, extending the interaction across successive video chunks.

How CoachWorld conditions the prediction

  1. 01

    Adapt native actions

    Frozen embodiment adapters convert each expert's native action chunk into a shared Cartesian end-effector trajectory.

  2. 02

    Unify embodiments

    Two arm slots encode position, 6D rotation, gripper state, and an existence mask. Franka uses one slot and bimanual robots use both.

  3. 03

    Ground every camera

    Intrinsics and extrinsics project future states into each view, while trajectory rasters and camera rays condition the video denoiser.

  4. 04

    Roll forward in closed loop

    Sparse history, instruction, and future state condition the next predicted chunk. Its final frame becomes the next policy observation.

Progress judge

Track progress and identify the first unfinished subtask

We use RoboMeter as the progress judge. For every routed subtask, the progress judge scores progress from the causal video prefix. A stage is confirmed only when its trace crosses a calibrated threshold. The first timeout identifies the active subtask–expert pair.

Live diagnosis stack_blocks_three RoboTwin · six routed stages

Reading the traceCompletion thresholds and deadlines are calibrated on held-out trajectories and then frozen. A threshold crossing returns control to the router. If a stage remains unfinished at its deadline, the system records the active subtask–expert pair. The video below shows the observed prefix. An unresolved stage in a shortened clip is labelled unconfirmed.

  1. 01

    Score the active stage

    At each frame, the progress judge estimates progress for the current routed subtask using only the rollout observed so far.

  2. 02

    Confirm each transition

    Crossing the calibrated 0.75 threshold confirms the active stage and moves the trace to the next subtask.

  3. 03

    Assign the first timeout

    P1–P5 cross threshold. P6 remains unresolved, localizing the failure to the active placement expert.

How reliable is the progress judge?

ResultsRoboMeter achieves 67.27% failed-subtask accuracy and 88.21% outcome F1, with a mean switch-timing error of 0.414 seconds. It performs best across all six reported metrics in this comparison.

EvaluationAll six judges receive the same causal reference-video replay, streaming protocol, and frozen routing. Successful trajectories test switch timing, failed trajectories test exact failed-subtask attribution, and both together test binary outcome prediction with failure as the positive class. Each method’s completion threshold is calibrated on the same held-out split and frozen for evaluation.
